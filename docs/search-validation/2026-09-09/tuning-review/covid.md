# COVID-19 historical search tuning review

Date: 2026-09-09  
Surface: `http://127.0.0.1:3081/api/search`  
Retrieval: hybrid; relevance order; 20 results returned per snapshot, with the first 10 judged here.

## Method and rubric

I used ten distinct historical COVID-19 queries with explicit years or dated events. For each query I first requested `freshness=0&votes=0`, retained the returned `session`, and then tried four signal settings against that same cached candidate set: `10/20`, `30/20`, `20/50`, and `50/10`. Thus the experiments isolate reranking from candidate retrieval. The baseline and the selected setting below are both scores for the displayed top ten.

Topic relevance: 1 means mostly unrelated, 3 mixed, and 5 almost entirely on-topic. Overall quality: 1 means unhelpful, 3 means usable but with substantial misses or noise, and 5 means a strong useful result set for the stated historical intent. These are editorial judgments: titles can be ambiguous, a story can be topically related while missing the requested date/event, and no article bodies were opened. “Preferred” means the most satisfactory of the tested orderings for the stated historical intent; it is not a learned optimum.

## Aggregate and caveats

Across the ten queries, baseline mean topic relevance was **4.5/5** and mean retrospective quality **3.9/5** (39 total quality points). The selected settings scored **4.6/5** for topic relevance and **4.1/5** for retrospective quality (41 total points). Lockdowns improved by one point and mRNA trials improved by one point; the other eight were unchanged. The selected settings were `20/50`, `50/10`, `50/10`, `20/50`, `10/20`, `0/0`, `50/10`, `10/20`, `30/20`, and `0/0` in query order.

Retrospective historical-topic usefulness is separate from strict dated intent. On that stricter reading, the preferred result set is satisfactory for onset (yes: 2020 and late-2019 onset evidence), Wuhan (yes), the WHO declaration (yes), mRNA trials (yes: direct 2020 trial results remain prominent), vaccine rollout (yes: the first two are December rollout/approval evidence), Delta (yes: 2021 evidence dominates), and Omicron (yes: November/December 2021 evidence dominates). Lockdowns are **no** for strict March 2020 intent: the preferred set is useful retrospective lockdown research, including the 2022 meta-analysis (30167436), but it does not consistently constrain results to March 2020. Long COVID is **no** for strict 2021–22 intent: it is an excellent retrospective topical set, but newer 2024–26 research appears among the highest-ranked results. School closures are **yes** for broad 2020 historical intent, though not a complete event chronology. These yes/no labels are qualitative and intentionally avoid pretending that title dates alone establish a precise publication window.

Freshness is a reranker over the frozen retrieved candidates, so it cannot recover a missing historical story. Votes can promote broadly popular COVID stories or cross-disease vaccine/variant stories; this was clearest for the mRNA and school queries. The 25-point eligibility floor, title/URL-only retrieval, duplicate headlines, HN title ambiguity, and the five-minute process-local session all limit this small subjective review. Scores judge titles and metadata only; they are not claims about the truth of any headline.
